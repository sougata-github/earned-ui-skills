## What is Liquid Glass?

Liquid Glass is the material Apple introduced in 2025 for iOS 26 and macOS Tahoe. It is a clear slab that bends the content behind it, the way a lens does, with light catching its rim. Apple defines it by that bending, which it calls lensing.

It sits on the functional layer of an interface: controls, bars and widgets floating over content. It is not meant for the content itself.

## Is Liquid Glass the same as glassmorphism?

No. Glassmorphism is a frosted look made with a backdrop blur, a border and a white tint. Liquid Glass refracts. The edge of the glass shows the background displaced and magnified, and the middle stays clear.

| | Glassmorphism | Liquid Glass |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it does to the background | Blurs it | Bends it at the edge |
| Made with | `backdrop-filter: blur()` | Refraction, in a shader |
| Edge | A border | A lit rim and a refracted band |
| Where the light is | Nowhere | One fixed direction for the page |

The skill's one rule follows from this: glass bends light, a blur scatters it. If there is no refraction at the edge, it is not done.

## How do you make a liquid glass effect on the web?

You draw it in WebGL2. One canvas draws the image and the glass together, and each glass element on the page is a transparent box whose position and corner radius are measured and sent to the shader.

For every pixel of a shape the shader works out how far it is from the edge, builds a surface with a flat top and a rounded lip, and bends the view through it with Snell's law. Red, green and blue bend by slightly different amounts, which gives a trace of colour at the rim. Frost, a fixed light, a rim highlight and a soft contact shadow are layered on top.

Text and controls stay real HTML over the canvas, so they remain selectable, accessible and sharp.

## How does the skill work?

liquid-glass-web is a workflow your agent follows, not a package. Ask for glass, for example "a liquid glass player widget over this image", and the agent works in a fixed order: what sits behind the glass, the architecture, a tuning lab, then one component at a time through that lab.

The lab is the method. Glass is judged by eye, and a description like "a bit thicker, less rim, softer light" does not survive the round trip through code and reloads. In the lab you drag a slider for every property and press Copy as JSON. The agent locks those values as the material for every component after it.

## What is in the skill?

- **A renderer** in one TypeScript file with no dependencies. It draws up to eight glass panels and four glass on glass insets, melts nearby shapes together, and cross-fades between images.
- **A tuning lab** in one React file, with a slider for every property and Copy as JSON.
- **The optics, term by term**, so the material can be tuned with intent and the shader extended.
- **Apple's rules, distilled**, with sources: where glass belongs, when to use the clear variant, why glass never sits on glass.
- **The lessons**: each failure met while shipping it, with its cause and fix.

## When is this not the right tool?

When the glass has to sit over live, arbitrary page content, such as a header over a scrolling article. The WebGL approach needs to own what is behind the glass. The skill covers the alternatives and their limits, but they are harder and less exact.

It is also the wrong tool for a page with no rich image behind the glass. Refraction is invisible over a flat colour.
